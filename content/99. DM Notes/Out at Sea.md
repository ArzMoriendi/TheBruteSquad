Excellent. This is the point where I would deliberately slow the game down again. The party has boarded the _Dawnmere_, they've met Captain Seryn, and now they have nowhere to go. For the first time in the campaign since leaving Mirabar, they are **truly committed** to a course of action. There are no roads to take, no towns to divert to, no mountains to climb around. There is only the sea.

This scene is about establishing life aboard the _Dawnmere_, allowing roleplay, introducing memorable crew members, and planting the seeds for Bronn's revelation. The pacing should feel peaceful and almost therapeutic, so that when the cliffhanger arrives later in the session, it feels like the calm before a storm.

## DM Notes

This scene is not about travel.

It is about routine.

Encourage the players to spend time talking with one another and with the crew. The ship should quickly begin to feel like a home. By the time it is threatened in Session 21, the players should care about saving it—and the people aboard.

## The Rhythm of the Ship

Over the next several hours, the party gradually settles into the cadence of shipboard life.

Crew members move with practiced precision.

- Sailors reef and trim the sails.
    
- A cook stirs a great pot of fish stew below deck.
    
- A young cabin boy scrubs the deck while humming a tune.
    
- The bosun quietly inspects every line twice.
    
- Captain Seryn rarely raises their voice, yet every command carries across the ship with effortless authority.
    

There is no wasted motion aboard the _Dawnmere_.

# Investigation

If the party insists on investigating the hold before nightfall, Bosun Garrik intercepts them.

> "Cargo manifest says passengers stay topside."
> 
> "Captain's orders."

He isn't hostile, merely firm.

A successful **Persuasion (DC 18)** allows them to accompany him briefly into the hold. They find tightly packed cargo, tarps, barrels, and crates—including one particularly large, iron-banded crate with small ventilation holes. It smells faintly of rot and seawater.

Inside are **live giant coconut crabs** being transported to Port Nyanzaru for a wealthy restaurateur. Their scraping claws and the odor of damp shellfish explain much of what the crew has heard.

However...

If **Bronn casts Detect Undead**, the spell reveals **no undead within the crate**.

Instead, its unmistakable presence radiates quietly from **Sarhice**.

Don't announce this to the table. Pass Bronn's player a note or take them aside for a private conversation. Let that revelation belong to the player before it belongs to the character. It creates a wonderful roleplaying dilemma: does Bronn confront Sarhice immediately, quietly watch, seek counsel, or keep the knowledge to himself?

## Transition

As darkness settles over the Trackless Sea, the lanterns are lit one by one across the _Dawnmere_. The ship creaks gently as it rides the swells, and the quiet murmur of the crew fades into the steady rhythm of wind and water.

Captain Seryn's final order echoes softly across the deck:

> "Night watches, relieve your stations."

The first stars emerge overhead.

One by one, the crew retires below.

And somewhere in the stillness of that first night at sea...

...another dream begins.
