A true hamlet on the Sword Coast should feel _small, practical, and dependent on nearby larger settlements_ like Neverwinter.

A hamlet is usually:

- 20–100 permanent residents
    
- Few or no formal government buildings
    
- Limited commerce
    
- One primary industry or reason for existing
    
- Residents know each other personally
    
- Travelers stop briefly rather than settle
    

For a hamlet just outside Neverwinter, Primcross likely exists because:

- it sits near a crossroads or bridge
    
- serves farmers, trappers, hunters, and caravans
    
- supports traffic heading toward Neverwinter
    
- offers rest before entering the city
    

That means you do **not** want:

- massive markets
    
- multiple competing inns
    
- specialized magic stores
    
- huge temples
    

Instead, think:

> “A place where travelers can survive a night.”

# 🌾 Primcross Overview

## Suggested Hamlet Flavor

Primcross sits where an old trade road crosses a narrow stream feeding toward the Neverwinter River. A few dozen buildings cluster around the crossing: timber homes, muddy lanes, fenced goat pens, smoke curling from chimneys, and lanterns swinging in coastal wind.

The people are:

- suspicious but practical
    
- used to travelers
    
- aware of Neverwinter politics but disconnected from them
    
- deeply interested in rumors

# 🧠 Veteran DM Advice

The best small settlements feel:

- personal
    
- grounded
    
- lived in
    

Players remember:

- recurring NPCs
    
- odd local customs
    
- one memorable building
    
- a strange rumor
    

Not population statistics.

For Primcross, I’d focus on:

1. The inn
    
2. The stable
    
3. One weird local mystery
    

That’s enough to make it feel real.

Aldrich Sternfollow
