I think we should end this session with **three scenes**, not one.

A published adventure has a rhythm to it:

- **Reflection**
    
- **Normalcy**
    
- **Dread**
    

Your players have now experienced Reflection (GhoulMan and Zwarg).

Now they need a little Normalcy before the Dread.

I also want the cliffhanger to feel **earned**. It shouldn't come out of nowhere—it should feel like something has slowly been creeping toward them since they left Neverwinter.

Here's how I'd close Session 20.

---

# Scene Eight

# A Routine at Sea

## DM Notes

This scene serves one purpose:

Convince the players that life aboard the _Dawnmere_ is becoming comfortable.

By now, everyone knows everyone's name.

The ship has become home.

That makes the next scene hurt.

---

## Read Aloud

> Morning comes with the smell of fresh bread and sea air.
> 
> Dawn breaks over the Trackless Sea in brilliant shades of orange and gold, the rising sun casting a glittering path across the endless water. The _Dawnmere_ rides the gentle swells with effortless grace, its timbers creaking softly as though the vessel itself has settled comfortably into the voyage.
> 
> Above, gulls have long since disappeared. Only the occasional flying fish breaks the surface before vanishing once more beneath the waves.
> 
> Breakfast is simple but hearty. Crew and passengers alike gather briefly before returning to their daily routines.
> 
> It is easy—dangerously easy—to believe the voyage will continue this way all the way to Chult.

They greet each sailor by name.

Notice small details.

Correct mistakes quietly.

# End of Session 20

## DM Notes

Don't ask for initiative.

Don't reveal how many pirates there are.

Don't place minis.

Don't answer tactical questions.

Let them spend the week discussing:

- Should they cut the grappling lines?
    
- Should they defend the rail?
    
- Should they board the pirate ship?
    
- Who's the pirate captain?
    
- Is this really just pirates?
    

Because...

It isn't.

## Session 21 Opening

The players will think they're starting a pirate battle.

They're wrong.

About three rounds into the boarding action, just as the tide begins to turn, introduce your second threat—the one that neither Captain Seryn nor the pirate captain anticipated. Whether it's a dragon turtle demanding tribute, an ancient sea predator disturbed by the battle, or another force of nature, the combat instantly shifts from "heroes versus pirates" to "everyone trying to survive."

That twist transforms the opening of Session 21 from a conventional boarding action into a memorable set piece and gives you a believable path to wrecking both ships, scattering survivors, and washing the party ashore in Chult with only what they can salvage from the sea. I think it's a much stronger opening than beginning with the disaster itself, because the players first get the excitement of a pirate battle before the campaign reminds them that, on the Trackless Sea, there are always bigger dangers lurking beneath the surface.
